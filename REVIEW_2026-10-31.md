# Review of the C40 El Niño outlook material

31 October 2026. Reviewed: `site/index.html`, the 98 JSON files under `site/data/`, and the tables under `data/processed/` (`linear_results`, `quantile_results`, `seasonal_panel`, `city_facts`) plus `data/raw/RONI.ascii.txt`.

## What checks out

We re-fitted the Nairobi Dec–Feb temperature regression from `seasonal_panel.csv` and reproduced every stored quantity to four decimals: both coefficients and their standard errors, the residual spread, the 2027 center and spread (residual plus parameter plus RONI variance), the 1991–2020 terciles, the three tercile probabilities, the effect ratio, and the years-of-warming equivalent. The Benjamini–Hochberg flags reproduce exactly as one family of 768 tests with the bar at p = 0.0106. The 192 paragraphs agree with the stored numbers wherever a sentence could be parsed: 182 trend rates, 305 chance percentages, the years-of-trend equivalents, and all 51 statements about the hot and cold tails, which appear only where the tail-difference p is below 0.09. The 96 cities match the roster on c40.org one for one. The site loads from a local server with no console errors, every tab and chart draws, and the map of effects is the textbook teleconnection pattern: warm and dry over southern Africa and northern South America, wet over East Africa and the US Gulf coast, warm over the Pacific Northwest and Southeast Asia, wet and humid over South China, nothing over Europe.

## What to fix, in order of importance

**The RONI assumption is undocumented and looks low.** The outlook assumes a Sep–Nov 2026 RONI of +2.2 with a standard deviation of 0.25. Neither `meta.json` nor the Methods page says where either number comes from. CPC's ENSO discussion of 8 October 2026 puts Niño-3.4 at +2.1 °C and gives a 54% chance that the Sep–Nov RONI reaches +2.5 or more. Under the site's assumption that chance is 11%. The largest Sep–Nov RONI in the 1951–2025 fit is +2.22 in 1997, so a season near +2.5 would put all 768 forecasts outside the fitted range and scale every effect by 1.14. State the source of the estimate in `meta.json` and in the Methods page, widen the spread to CPC's, and re-export when CPC publishes the Aug–Oct value in early November. The next CPC discussion is dated 12 November 2026.

**The outlook uses the El Niño term at full weight whatever its confidence.** The 2027 center is a + c·(2027 − t̄) + b·2.2 for all 768 cells, including the roughly 400 where b is indistinguishable from zero. Thirteen directional chances of 60% or more come mainly from an El Niño term labelled Weak, Detectable or none, and four of them are quoted in paragraphs: Delhi Mar–May rain, Fuzhou Mar–May rain, Mumbai Dec–Feb rain, and Santiago Dec–Feb rain. Mumbai Dec–Feb reads "very little rain falls ... with no clear El Niño link" and two sentences later "a wetter-than-normal season is likely (about 70% chance)", on a 7 mm normal. Shrink b toward zero, with a hierarchical prior across cities or simply b = 0 below p < 0.05, and let the paragraph say the outlook rests on the trend alone in those cells. The same change removes the three paragraphs that say "mostly because of the long-term warming trend" where the El Niño term is in fact the larger part: Ahmedabad Mar–May, Chengdu Dec–Feb, Chicago Dec–Feb.

**The confidence ladder promises more than the p-values deliver.** The 198 tests with p ≥ 0.5 imply about 396 null tests among the 768. Spreading those uniformly over the other bins gives the share of each label that is likely noise:

| Label | p-value | Observed | Expected if null | Likely false |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong | < 0.01 | 173 | 4 | 2% |
| Clear | 0.01–0.05 | 93 | 16 | 17% |
| Modest | 0.05–0.10 | 69 | 20 | 29% |
| Detectable | 0.10–0.20 | 87 | 40 | 46% |
| Weak | 0.20–0.50 | 148 | 119 | 80% |

The paragraphs render Clear and Modest with the same phrase, "modest but consistent", and render Detectable as "detectable but uncertain" or "an uncertain hint". Half of the Detectable effects are noise. Reduce the ladder to four tiers: Strong (survives FDR), Clear (p < 0.05), Suggestive (p < 0.10), and no identifiable effect. Reserve "consistent" for Strong. The map and the cover already treat Detectable and Weak as no effect, so only the chips and the prose change.

**The map legend and the code disagree.** The legend at `site/index.html:371` says the dark dots are "strong or clear", and the cover count at line 327 adds Strong and Clear (39 of 96 for Dec–Feb). `CONF_TIER` at line 255 puts Clear in tier 2, so the 11 Clear cities draw in the soft color labelled "modest" and only the 28 Strong cities draw dark. Either set Clear to 1 or relabel the legend "strong" and "clear or modest".

**Two station-check promises in Methods are not kept.** Santiago Mar–May temperature falls by 0.12 °C per decade in ERA5 with p = 0.005. The paragraph says "no clear long-term trend" and nothing about a missing station record, while Methods says the description says so. Dhaka Mar–May quotes the ERA5 humidity trend of +0.7 points per decade, while Methods says the station does not confirm Dhaka humidity. Methods also asserts that "weather stations agree well with ERA5 on year-to-year swings and on the direction of El Niño effects", and nothing in the folder backs that sentence. Fix the two paragraphs and either add the station comparison or delete the sentence.

**Dry seasons get rainfall statements that mean nothing.** Eleven city-seasons have a 1991–2020 rainfall normal under 30 mm. The Gaussian tercile model and the percentage trend break down there. Dakar Mar–May has a 1 mm normal and is given "a modest but consistent effect toward a drier season with fewer rainy days". Below a threshold near 25 mm, report the normal and stop: no El Niño sentence and no rainfall outlook.

**Chance wording rounds one way and the number another.** "More likely than not (about 50%)" appears seven times (Austin Mar–May, Dalian Dec–Feb, Guangzhou Mar–May, Istanbul Dec–Feb, New York City Dec–Feb, Paris Mar–May, Rotterdam Mar–May) and "slightly favored (about 50%)" four times, because the phrase is picked on the unrounded value. Write "about even odds" for 45–55%.

**The folder cannot regenerate itself.** The page's error text at line 276 points to `code/06_export_site_data.py`, `meta.json` names `docs/city_paragraphs_DRAFT.md`, and Methods names `C40_ENSO_metrics.xlsx`. None of the three is in the folder, and we found no copy elsewhere on this machine. Add the code, the paragraph source, and a README with the run order before this goes anywhere.

**The quantile bootstrap has unconverged fits.** Each cell runs 303 fits and 31 to 44 of them hit the iteration cap, 10 to 15%. Methods should say whether those fits are kept. If they are, the tail-difference p-values are slightly off.

**Smaller items.** The Mar–May outlook uses Sep–Nov RONI at six months' lead when the Dec–Feb value will be known before Mar–May starts; say the outlook will be refreshed then. "Three months ahead of Dec–Feb" is center to center; the Sep–Nov value is published after Dec–Feb begins, as the paragraphs' last sentence already admits. `site/.ipynb_checkpoints/` and `data/.DS_Store` would ship with the site. The `claude:viewport-fit-sides` meta tag at line 8 is an artifact leftover. The expression `has_paragraph ? "" : ""` at line 409 does nothing.
